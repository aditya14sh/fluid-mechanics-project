About the project

Water pushes on a curved surface in two ways. The horizontal force equals the push on the gate's vertical shadow, and the vertical force equals the weight of the water sitting above the gate. Because pressure always acts at right angles to the arc, the resultant passes straight through the centre of the circle.

Our rig turns this into something you can see. Two strings run from the gate over five pulleys to hanging cups: one pulls horizontally to balance FH, the other vertically to balance FV. With 400 g and 628 g in the cups, the full tank is held in balance. We also checked the five course cases, from a 0.5 m to a 1.5 m gate.

How the forces are found

Assumptions: fluid at rest (hydrostatic pressure only), gate sides sealed, water level falls to the gate edge if the gate opens past it, gate buoyancy neglected, frictionless hinge and pulleys. Pressure is integrated numerically over the gate in its current position, so results stay valid while it swings. Rig geometry from our v14 drawings.
